# Spec — approval is a job state, not a side feature

**Contract.** Approval is modeled as states of the job itself:
`pending-approval → running → done | denied | killed | timed-out`. A command that
matches no rule is registered as **pending-approval** and returns its id *without
spawning*. The human's decision transitions the state; an **edited-argv** approval
writes the corrected argv into the job as a structured correction back-channel.

**Chosen.** One state machine. Notification, correction loops, and "why was this
rejected" all fall out of the states — pending jobs are simply *visible* on the
console like any other job.

**Rejected.** Bolting approval on as an out-of-band mechanism separate from the job
lifecycle. That splits observability and loses the correction/audit path.

**Escape hatch.** Edit-and-approve is the in-band correction channel: the human
fixes the argv and approves in one action, and the corrected argv is what runs and
what is audited.
