# Spec — the rule-set is target-node-owned, human-only-mutated, fail-closed

**Contract.** The allow/deny rule-set lives on and is owned by the **target
node's** daemon. A requesting agent may *propose* and *run*, but can **never** add,
remove, or widen a rule — only a human, at the console, mutates it. There is **no
agent-callable method that writes a rule.** A proposal matching no rule
**fails closed**: it becomes pending, and if no console is connected or no human
decides within a timeout, the run is **denied** (never run, never a silent hang),
with an explicit "denied: no approver / timed out" returned to the agent.

**Chosen.** Two layers over one matcher — a **git-committed seed** (reviewed in
VCS) and a **daemon-owned mutable overlay** written *only* in response to human
approval decisions. The agent gets run + read-only introspection; seeing what a
human already granted is not the power to grant.

**Rejected.** A rule-set the requester can seed or extend (agent supplies its own
allow-list, or auto-allow-on-first-use). The requester is the *untrusted*
principal; letting it escalate collapses the entire trust boundary.

**Escape hatch.** Granting new authority is always a human action at the console
(§2.3); the seed layer handles recurring parameterized needs via reviewed recipes.
