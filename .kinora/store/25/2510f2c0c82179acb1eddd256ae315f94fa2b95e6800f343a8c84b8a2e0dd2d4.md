# Task — supervise long-running processes on the job substrate

Realize supervision of long-running dev processes — TUIs, watchers, dev servers —
as the **`service` half of the one job substrate**, not as a parallel subsystem.
This is the umbrella tracking item; a v1 scope and phase plan are defined in a later
design round (no plan kino yet).

## Done-when

- A declared `service` job runs under supervision: manifest-declared, gated by the
  node's rule-set on the same path as any governed command, restarted per policy,
  with its port allocated by the node and visible in every listing.
- Both lenses work: a TUI is readable as a faithful VT screen by a human and as a
  declared machine view by an agent, with the agent read cheap enough to be used.
- The registry survives a daemon restart, re-adopts its live jobs, and never kills
  a job because a daemon went away.
- `ensure` is idempotent and silent: it starts what is declared for this session,
  skips what a gate refuses, and never stops or restarts anything.
- Every refusal names the gate that refused and why; no silent skip on an explicit
  request.
- Each realizing module cites its spec: `// implements: <spec-id>`.

## Known-open, to decide before or during the plan

- **Who owns a `terminal`-lens job's PTY master** — the honest gap recorded in
  kino://a8f6cfd51b1c823d4f9331e01962f7c2b88976587cff32120f3ce98a803c24c7. A
  transient init scope would solve durability and resource limits together but
  loses VT fidelity; the choice decides whether a per-job component returns.
- **Resource limits stay advisory.** Sampling, history and threshold events are in
  scope; throttling and killing are not. Limit fields belong in the schema from day
  one so enforcement is purely additive.
- **The agent face** (a stdio MCP shim, cwd as the workspace key, one parameterized
  multi-node tool surface) lands in `kudo`, not here, and needs its own spec once a
  daemon exists to talk to.
