# Spec — intervention is the human's only write, and is minimized

**Contract.** Awareness is always-on: the human may observe anything, anytime. But
the human *writes* only at genuine gates — **approve / deny (including
edit-and-approve)**, **kill / abort**, and **take over** (typing into a
terminal job, as at its keyboard). There is no other human write path into the
substrate, and the system is designed to need as *few* of these as possible.

Take over is the human's alone: a session may not type into a job, not even
one of its own workspace, since typing reaches past the approval an argv was
given. Each job's audit record counts the inputs it received
(kino://4ef2467e0ecf835f0f47575d78eefbb89fc13f4b15c68d4ff992c1942b985b9e).

Approve / deny crosses the job connector as take over does: `decide` is
answered for the console caller only, and refused for any session, so a human
away from the node (on a phone, through a console) can answer the gate while
no agent can answer its own request. The jobs awaiting a verdict are a
read (`pending`), like every other observation.

**Chosen.** A tiny, well-defined write surface (three gates) plus governance policy
that keeps the agent proceeding on its own. The number of gates a human must pass
is driven down by design, not by convention.

**Rejected.** Treating the human as a co-operator with broad write access, or
gating routine steps. Either turns the supervisor back into a bottleneck and
dilutes which writes actually matter.

**Escape hatch.** Kill/abort is always available as the human's unconditional
override on any running job, independent of the approval gate.
