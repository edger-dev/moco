# Spec — the job substrate is realized as a moco cell layer over the hub

**Contract.** The job substrate is built in **moco**, not in the hub. It is
realized as moco **cell(s)** whose **Funcs** are the job surface —
`start` / `tail` / `wait` / `kill` / `preflight` / `run` (with `run` as
`start` + `wait` sugar) — carrying `FuncSpec` metadata like any other moco
function. The substrate **rides on the hub** for transport and node routing only:
the hub relays connectors and knows nothing of jobs, rule-sets, approvals, or
audit. The hub boundary stays swappable; moco binds to the hub's published
protocol, never to node-local internals.

**Chosen.** Put the job substrate in the moco layer as composable cells over the
moco `Cell`/`Func` model, with the `Env` trait supplying the runtime context a cell
needs (registry handle, node identity, rule-set/audit backends). This keeps the
substrate a first-class moco capability others compose with, and keeps the hub thin
(pure relay).

**Rejected.** Implementing the job substrate inside the hub (the hub is transport +
topology + deploy; teaching it jobs collapses the boundary the hub brief protects);
and building it as a bespoke service outside moco's cell/func model (loses
composability and the typed-spec surface moco exists to provide).

**Escape hatch.** A capability the moco↔hub protocol cannot yet express rides the
hub's opaque routed-call payload until it earns a first-class method — the hub gains
no job-specific surface.
